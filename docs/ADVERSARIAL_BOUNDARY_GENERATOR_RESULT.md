# Adversarial boundary generator: frozen selection gate fails

**Result date:** 16 October 2026  
**Status:** **FAIL — insufficient eligible cases before outcome opening**  
**Classification:** prospective truth-blind generator failure

## Smallest supported claim

The frozen four-base response-perturbation generator cannot produce its
preregistered 24-case adversarial manifest. Both three-dimensional bases chose
the candidate solver at every one of 97 perturbations, leaving zero eligible
independent-lower-CV cases. The two five-dimensional bases produced 37 and 49
eligible cases, but the frozen cap of six per base limits the valid manifest to
at most 12 cases.

Therefore the study fails before any extrapolation truth is evaluated. It
provides no safety evidence for or against the prediction-envelope certificate.

## Residual and regime split

The failure is not uniform. Independent-lower-CV opportunities were absent in
3-D (0/194) but common in 5-D (86/194, 44.33%). This is an opened,
training-only dimension split, not a confirmed scaling law. It weakens the
assumption that one fixed perturbation direction generates solver-boundary
crossings uniformly across dimensions.

On the 3-D seed-5107 shard, normalized prediction RMS disagreement remained on
the order of (10^{-7}) throughout the grid, consistent with benign
implementation equivalence rather than a basin crossing. Outcome truth was not
needed for this diagnosis.

## CONSEQUENCE ledger

Do not join extrapolation truth or evaluate the partial 12-case set as if it
satisfied the frozen protocol. The envelope receipt remains experimental and
unpromoted.

The cheapest next discrimination is a **new, separately frozen** generator on
new 5-D/7-D bases, explicitly testing the mechanism-derived prediction that
eligible boundary frequency rises with dimension. It must retain the same
envelope thresholds and must commit a complete truth-free manifest before any
outcome join. If that second generator yields fewer than four harmful
opportunities, the preregistered kill condition retires the pairwise-envelope
kernel.
