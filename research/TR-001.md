# Kernellum Research Technical Report 001

## Kernellum Compiler v0.1: Constraint-Driven Generation of a Quantized Neural Accelerator

**Sushanth Dasari — Kernellum Research**  
Version 1.0 · September 2026

**PDF:** [`TR-001.pdf`](TR-001.pdf)

### Abstract

Kernellum Compiler v0.1 is a compact research prototype for model-to-accelerator generation. It trains a small neural workload, constructs an integer-only representation, evaluates candidate MAC-lane configurations against a latency constraint, emits FPGA-oriented SystemVerilog and memory initialization files, and verifies the generated design against a separate cycle-accurate software model. On a 64→32→16→10 handwritten-digit classifier, the floating-point model reaches **96.22%** held-out accuracy and the generated INT8 execution model reaches **96.44%**; their predictions agree on **99.78%** of 450 held-out samples. The cycle-level model matches the vectorized INT8 output tensor exactly on **450/450** samples. The generated RTL passes **32/32** held-out golden-vector simulations under Icarus Verilog and synthesizes successfully under Yosys 0.33 with **0 CHECK problems**. Under an assumed 100 MHz clock and a ≤10 µs modeled core-latency constraint, the architecture search selects four parallel MAC lanes, corresponding to **680 modeled compute cycles / 6.8 µs**. Physical FPGA implementation remains future work.

## Workload

- Dataset: `sklearn.datasets.load_digits`
- Network: 64 → 32 → 16 → 10
- Hidden activation: ReLU
- Held-out samples: 450
- Float accuracy: **96.22%**
- INT8 accuracy: **96.44%**
- Float/INT8 prediction agreement: **99.78%**

## Architecture search

| MAC lanes | Core cycles | Modeled latency @ 100 MHz | Meets ≤10 µs |
|---:|---:|---:|:---:|
| 1 | 2720 | 27.2 µs | no |
| 2 | 1360 | 13.6 µs | no |
| 4 | 680 | 6.8 µs | yes — selected |
| 8 | 340 | 3.4 µs | yes |
| 16 | 170 | 1.7 µs | yes |

The latency values are cycle-count models at an assumed clock. They are **not** measured FPGA timings.

## Claim boundary

v0.1 does not yet establish physical FPGA timing closure, named-device resource utilization, measured power, measured end-to-end latency, arbitrary ONNX support, transformer operators, or competitive superiority over established accelerator compilers.

## Next milestone

v0.2 begins ONNX graph lowering, an explicit hardware IR and a named Lattice ECP5-85F target profile. The next evidence threshold after family-mapped synthesis is place-and-route on a named board followed by measured latency and power.
